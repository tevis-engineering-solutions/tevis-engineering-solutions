Instant Roof Estimates, On Your Own Site

ShingleAI is a roof-estimate tool that lives on your roofing website, under your name. A homeowner types their address; the roof is measured straight from aerial survey data — real area, real pitch, nothing to draw — and they see a price band built from your rate card. The lead lands in your inbox with the measurement, your internal cost breakdown, and a reply you can send from the truck. Your estimator, your brand, your lead.

Your estimator, on your site — not a lead marketplace

The homeowner never leaves your website and never goes through a middleman. They get a real number in about a minute, you get a lead that was never sold to anyone else, and the first call starts at “when can you come measure it” instead of “what do you charge.”

Every estimate ends the same way: this is an estimate, not a quote, and the only way to know is to look. That is deliberate. The tool exists to book the site visit — warm, with the roof already measured — not to price a job off a photograph.

What it does

Measured from survey data

Where coverage exists, the roof's pitch-corrected area and its real pitch come straight from aerial survey data, plane by plane. No drawing, no guessing, and no “about how steep is it?” from someone who has never been on a ladder.

The real roof outline

The outline is traced from the imagery itself and drawn on the aerial, so it looks like the house. If it cannot be traced cleanly, no outline is drawn at all — a wrong picture on the one screen that says “that's my house” is worse than none.

Pitch correction built in

An aerial shows a footprint, not a roof. A 6/12 roof is 11.8% bigger than its footprint; a 12/12 is 41.4% bigger. Every estimate applies that correction, then squares, waste, tear-off per layer, and adders for steep pitch, height, and layers.

Your rate card stays private

The page sends geometry to the server; the server sends back dollars. Your rates never reach the browser, so no competitor reads your margins out of view-source — and the estimator only runs on domains you own.

A lead email you can act on

Not just a name. You get the contact, the measurement, a breakdown of your own cost on that roof, the aerial with the outline, a measured-or-traced badge, and a suggested reply with a one-tap button to send it.

Whitelabeled to you

Your logo, your colors, your phone number, your inbox, your materials, your rates. Homeowners see your company the whole way through — the software never asks for the spotlight.

How it works

Four steps. The homeowner is done in about a minute; you are done when you pick up the phone.

They type an address

On your site, in your branding. Suggestions come from Google Places, and an address typed straight through without picking a suggestion still resolves.

The roof gets measured

Survey data returns the pitch-corrected area, the measured pitch, and the plane count, and the outline is drawn on the aerial. No coverage? They trace it by hand instead.

Three questions

Material, stories, and how many layers are up there — the things no aerial can answer. Pitch is already filled in whenever it was measured.

Band, then booking

They see a price range and the accuracy notice, then leave their details to book a technician. The lead is in your inbox before they close the tab.

Why it says “estimate, not a quote” right under the number. Unusual shapes, dormers, and multi-level sections do not always resolve cleanly; trees, shadows, and snow hide roof; the imagery may be a year or two old; and nothing overhead shows the decking, the flashing, the ventilation, or how many layers are already up there. The widget says all of that in plain language, directly beneath the price, and adapts it to whether the roof was measured or hand-traced. A homeowner told the truth up front is not surprised by the real number — and that is the difference between a signed job and a cancelled appointment.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does the homeowner have to draw their own roof?
Usually not. When aerial survey data covers the building, the roof is measured for them — pitch-corrected area, the measured pitch, and the number of roof planes — and the outline is drawn on the satellite view so they only have to confirm it is the right house. Hand-tracing is the fallback for buildings the survey does not cover, or for anyone who says it is not their house.
How accurate is the estimate?
Accurate enough to price a conversation, not to sign a contract — and the widget says so directly under the number. It corrects the footprint to the true roof surface using the measured pitch (a 6/12 roof is 11.8% larger than its footprint; a 12/12 is 41.4% larger), and it excludes flat areas it believes are decks or porches and tells the homeowner it did. It cannot see decking, flashing, ventilation, or how many layers are already up there, so the output is deliberately a range and every path ends on booking an on-site measurement.
Will it work on my existing website?
In most cases, yes. The estimator is delivered as a responsive iframe plus a small sizing script, which works on WordPress, Squarespace, Wix, and custom sites. TES handles the install on your site rather than leaving you a code snippet to figure out.
Who owns the leads?
You do. Leads are emailed straight to your inbox from your own branded estimator on your own website. They are not sold, shared, or resold to other contractors in your market — which is the whole point of running your own estimator instead of buying from a lead marketplace.
Can my competitors see my pricing?
No. Your rate card never reaches the browser. The page sends the roof geometry to the server and the server sends back dollars, so there is nothing to read in view-source. Your estimator is also locked to your own domains, so nobody can embed it on another site and collect your leads.
What does it cost?
It is quoted per contractor. Two things drive the number: how complex your rate card and configuration are, and where your website is hosted — some hosts make the install a short job and others take more work. Google Maps and Solar API usage is billed to your own Google Cloud account at cost, on keys set up in your name with restrictions and a spending cap you control.
What happens if a roof is not covered by the survey data?
The estimator falls back to a hand trace: the homeowner outlines the roof on the aerial and picks a pitch, and the footprint is corrected to a surface area from there. Every lead is labeled measured or traced — in the app, in the lead record, and in your email — so you always know whether you are looking at survey data or a homeowner's best guess before you drive out.
