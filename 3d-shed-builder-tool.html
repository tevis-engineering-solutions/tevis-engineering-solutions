Let Them Build It Before They Call You

3D Shed Builder is a shed configurator that lives on your website, under your name. A customer picks a style, a size, colors, doors and windows, and watches the building assemble in 3D while an itemized price and a monthly payment update on every click. When they are happy, the design lands in your inbox as a lead — with the price they saw and a link that reopens their exact building. And unlike the platforms that rent you a configurator by the month, you buy this one once and own it, price sheet and all.

Your configurator, on your site — not a monthly platform fee

Most shoppers want a number before they want a salesperson. Right now they get one from whoever answers that question first. This puts the answer on your own site, in your own colors, at two in the morning — and a customer who has already built a shed on your website has picked out the doors before you ever speak.

The big configurator platforms solve this too, and they charge a subscription per dealer for the privilege — which is easy to justify at twenty lots and hard to justify at two. This is the buy-it-once version: one build, installed on your host, with a price sheet you control. There is nothing to renew and nobody to ask for permission when your lumber cost moves.

Three building styles, your sizes

A utility gable, a lofted barn gambrel, and a lean-to. Within each one, the sizes you sell, the wall heights you offer, and the roof geometry itself are yours to set — a taller barn or a shallower utility roof is a price-sheet edit, not a development project.

What needs a developer, honestly: anything that changes the plan of the building rather than its elevation — a wrapped porch, a side-lofted dormer. Those are a code change and we quote them separately, rather than pretending a price-sheet edit can produce them.

What it does for the sale

It builds while they watch

The model rebuilds on every change — move a door, swap to a gambrel roof, go from 7ft walls to 8ft — so the customer is designing, not filling in a form. That is what keeps them on the page long enough to send it.

An itemized price, not a mystery

The panel opens to show the line items: base building, siding upgrade, roof, each door and window, each add-on, and the regional adjustment. Customers trust a number they can take apart.

A monthly payment up front

Most of this market buys on rent-to-own, so the monthly figure sits above the cash price, computed from payment factors you set. It is a display, not a credit offer — no application, no lender, nothing implied about approval.

Real dimensioned drawings

A drawings tab gives front, back and both sides, dimensioned, with every opening located. It prints as a spec sheet — and it is what the small share of visitors with no working 3D see instead of a black rectangle.

Shareable design links

Every design has its own link. A customer texts it to a spouse, or you text it back after a phone call, and it reopens exactly the building they built — colors, doors, windows and all.

Rules it cannot break

A roll-up door needs 8ft walls; a loft kit only exists on a barn. The builder enforces that instead of letting someone design a building you cannot deliver — and it says why, in plain English.

How a customer uses it

Pick a style and a size

Utility, lofted barn or lean-to, then a size and wall height from the list you actually sell. The price appears immediately — there is no “submit to see pricing” wall.

Choose materials and colors

Siding and roof material, then the colors that material genuinely comes in. Pick vinyl and the barn-red option disappears, because it is not a color you can hand them.

Place doors, windows and add-ons

Openings go on a specific wall at a specific position, and the builder checks each one will fit under the roof there. Interior, electrical and exterior add-ons are itemized as they are ticked.

Send it to you

Name, email, phone and delivery ZIP. The lead reaches your inbox with the whole design, the price they saw, and a link that reopens it exactly — and they get their own copy of the link.

The drawings are the same building as the 3D

Most configurators render a picture. This one derives both the 3D model and the dimensioned elevations from a single description of the building, so the drawings cannot show a door the model does not have, or a wall height the price did not charge for. It is the difference between a marketing render and something you would hand a crew.

You edit your own price sheet

This is the part that matters twelve months from now. Everything you sell — the styles, the sizes, the colors, the doors and windows, the add-ons, the prices, and the rules about what goes with what — lives in a small set of plain files on your own host. You edit a price, save, and the next customer sees the new number.

  • Wall-height adders per style

The validator is the safety net. Before you publish, one command checks the whole price sheet for the things a spot-check misses — a size you advertise but never priced, an option pointing at a style you deleted, a rule that only works in one direction. It reports them in plain English. And if a bad edit somehow reaches the site anyway, the builder refuses to start and shows the same plain list rather than a broken page in front of a customer.

Common questions

Who changes the prices when my costs go up?
You do, and it does not involve us. Your styles, sizes, colors, options, prices and rules live in a small set of plain files on your own host. You edit a price, save, and the next customer to load the builder sees the new number — no rebuild, no deploy, no support ticket. A validator is included that checks the whole price sheet before you publish and tells you in plain English if something does not line up, such as a size you advertise but never priced.
Is the price the customer sees a real quote?
It is an estimate, and the builder says so on screen. It prices the building from your own price sheet, but it does not know the delivery distance to that address, the site prep the lot needs, or local permitting — so every path ends on you confirming the number. That is deliberate. The tool exists to start a real conversation with someone who has already picked a building and seen a payment, not to sell a shed unattended.
What building styles does it handle?
Three ship out of the box: a utility gable, a lofted barn gambrel, and a lean-to. Within each one you control the sizes, wall heights and roof geometry, so a taller barn or a shallower utility roof is a price-sheet edit rather than a code change. Shapes that change the plan of the building rather than its elevation — a wrapped porch, a side-lofted dormer — are a code change, and we quote those separately.
Will it work on my existing website?
In most cases yes. The builder is a static site, so it lives at a page on your own domain such as yoursite.com/build and runs on ordinary web hosting — WordPress, Squarespace, Wix, and custom sites included. Tevis Engineering Solutions handles the install on your site rather than handing you a code snippet to figure out.
Where do the leads go?
Straight to you. When a customer sends their design, the lead is emailed to your inbox, or posted to a webhook so it can land in a CRM, a spreadsheet, or an automation. The email carries who they are, what they built, the price they saw, how to reach them, and a link that reopens their exact design. The leads are yours alone — not sold, shared, or resold to another dealer.
Does the 3D model show every option I sell?
No, and that is on purpose. Doors, windows, sizes, wall heights, materials and colors are drawn on the building. Add-ons such as insulation, electrical packages, shelving, ramps and anchor kits are priced and itemized but not modeled, because drawing them adds nothing to a customer's decision. Everything selected appears in the itemized price whether it is drawn or not.
What about the rent-to-own payment it shows?
It is a display, not a credit offer. It is calculated from payment factors you enter in your own price sheet so it mirrors the way your rent-to-own provider quotes. Nothing is submitted to a lender, no application is taken, and no approval is implied. If you do not sell rent-to-own, remove the terms and the builder shows the cash price alone.
What does it cost?
It is quoted per dealer. Two things drive the number: how large and how custom your catalog is — how many styles, sizes, options and rules you sell — and where your website is hosted, since some hosts make the install a short job and others take more work. You can buy it once and own the build outright, or have Tevis Engineering Solutions host and maintain it for you.
